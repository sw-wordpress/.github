# WordPress plugins, without the subscription circus 🎪

**Practical plugins. Open source. No unnecessary paywalls.**

Hi there! 👋

I've been building WordPress websites for almost 20 years. That's long enough to remember quite a few dashboard redesigns, more plugin conflicts than I'd like, and a time when finding a useful plugin didn't necessarily involve choosing a monthly subscription.

Over the past few years, finding solid plugins for fairly basic functionality has become surprisingly difficult. Not because the plugins don't exist, but because a feature that used to be straightforward can now come with a €10–20 monthly price tag.

One subscription might not hurt. A handful of them on every small business website? That adds up quickly.

## Why I'm building these plugins

After years of working with WordPress, I usually have a pretty clear idea of what I need a plugin to do — and, just as importantly, what I *don't* need it to do.

So I started using different AI tools to help turn those ideas into technical concepts and, eventually, working plugins. AI helps with the implementation; experience, testing, and real-world requirements decide what belongs in the product.

The goal isn't to reinvent everything or build the world's 47th all-in-one solution. It's to create useful tools that solve specific problems without unnecessary complications.

## This isn't a crusade against commercial plugins

Commercial developers deserve to be paid for their work. Good support, reliable maintenance, and software that just works are worth money.

I also understand why larger organizations pay for commercial products: they need help with setup, someone to call when things go wrong, and confidence that business-critical features will keep working. Whether a higher price always delivers that confidence is another discussion.

But I think it's fair to ask:

**Do I really need to charge every tiny business €500 a year just so its website can continue receiving updates?**

Yes, many plugins offer free versions. In practice, though, essential features may be available while the interface, workflows, integrations, or convenience features are deliberately restricted.

And updates aren't an optional luxury. Especially for software exposed to the internet, **security and maintenance updates are a must**, not a premium feature.

That tension is a big part of why this organization exists.

## Keep it useful, not endlessly bigger

I'd love contributions, feedback, and real-world testing. You don't have to write code to help — **a clear GitHub issue is already a valuable contribution**.

There is, however, one thing I want to avoid: *feature creep*.

Not every special requirement needs another checkbox, settings page, or submenu. Whenever possible, I'd rather keep the core simple and offer **hooks and extension points** for project-specific behavior.

In short: flexible enough for developers, understandable enough for everyone else. Ideally without needing a user manual the size of a phone book.

## Open source means open for business, too

The plugins in this organization are intended to be released under the **MIT License**. That means you're welcome to use, modify, redistribute, and incorporate them into commercial projects, subject to the license terms.

I also believe AI-assisted code should be shared openly wherever practical. AI systems have benefited from an enormous amount of human-created knowledge and content. Making useful results available to others feels like a reasonable way to give something back.

That's a personal principle, not a claim that AI-generated code is automatically free of copyright or licensing obligations. Dependencies and third-party code still need to be handled properly.

## Want to help?

Try a plugin. Report a bug. Suggest an improvement. Open a pull request. Or just tell me whether something works well in the real world.

Constructive feedback and contributions are always welcome.

**Donations? No thanks.** 🐾

I'm not accepting donations for these projects. If you feel like giving something back, your local animal shelter would probably appreciate the support far more than my GitHub account would.

Thanks for stopping by — and happy building!
