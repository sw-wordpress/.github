# WordPress plugins, without the subscription circus 🎪

**Practical plugins. Open source. No unnecessary paywalls.**

Hi there! 👋

I've been building WordPress websites for almost 20 years. That's long enough to remember quite a few dashboard redesigns, more plugin conflicts than I'd like, and a time when finding a useful plugin didn't necessarily involve choosing a monthly subscription.

Over the past few years, finding solid plugins for fairly basic functionality has become surprisingly difficult. Not because the plugins don't exist, but because a feature that used to be straightforward can now come with a €10–20 monthly price tag.

One subscription might not hurt. A handful of them on every small business website? That adds up quickly.

## Why I'm building these plugins

These days, WordPress development is mostly a hobby for me. I enjoy using it to help local clubs, non-profits, and small businesses get useful websites without turning every project into a major investment.

That also changes the economics. If you're running an agency with dozens of clients, a big "unlimited sites" license can make perfect sense: spread the cost across enough projects and everyone wins. But when you're helping a handful of organizations in your spare time, those agency bundles aren't always an option. I don't want to buy an expensive package upfront on the off chance that enough future projects will justify it. My hobby doesn't need a subscription business plan of its own.

After years of working with WordPress, I usually have a pretty clear idea of what I need a plugin to do — and, just as importantly, what I *don't* need it to do.

These projects are also my way of exploring what AI can actually make possible. I use different AI tools to turn my own ideas into technical concepts and, eventually, plugins that work the way *I* want to use them — rather than adapting my workflow to someone else's pricing tiers or product roadmap.

I believe AI, when guided with clear goals, technical understanding, and a healthy dose of critical thinking, can help **democratize access to knowledge and software development**. Ideas that once required a considerable budget or a whole development team can become realistic projects for individuals and small organizations. That's pretty exciting — even if it might make the occasional subscription sales department a little nervous.

AI is a powerful tool, not a magic "make it good" button. Experience, testing, and real-world requirements still decide what belongs in the product.

The goal isn't to reinvent everything or build the world's 47th all-in-one solution. It's to create useful tools that solve specific problems without unnecessary complications.

## This isn't a crusade against commercial plugins

Commercial developers deserve to be paid for their work. Good support, reliable maintenance, and software that just works are worth money.

I also understand why larger organizations pay for commercial products: they need help with setup, someone to call when things go wrong, and confidence that business-critical features will keep working. Whether a higher price always delivers that confidence is another discussion.

But I think it's fair to ask:

**Does every tiny business or local club really need to spend €500 a year just so its website can continue receiving updates?**

Yes, many plugins offer free versions. In practice, though, essential features may be available while the interface, workflows, integrations, or convenience features are deliberately restricted.

And updates aren't an optional luxury. Especially for software exposed to the internet, **security and maintenance updates are a must**, not a premium feature.

That tension is a big part of why this organization exists.

## AI-assisted doesn't mean quality-optional

Using AI to help write code doesn't lower the bar. If anything, it makes being deliberate about quality even more important.

My priorities are **code quality, meaningful tests, and usability**. A plugin should be reliable, easy to understand, and pleasant to use — not just technically capable of doing the job.

And just because code was generated with AI doesn't mean it should look like it was written for another AI to decipher. **Humans still need to be able to read, review, debug, maintain, and extend it.** Clear structure, sensible naming, consistent coding standards, and understandable implementation choices matter.

AI can save time writing code. It doesn't replace the responsibility to understand what the code does or to make sure it actually works. Preferably before a user discovers the interesting edge cases for us.

## Keep it useful, not endlessly bigger

I'd love feedback, real-world testing, and new ideas. You don't need to write code by hand to contribute — in fact, I'd encourage you to **use your own AI tools to turn your ideas into working improvements**. Bring your vision, guide the implementation, test the result, and share it. A well-described issue is welcome too, but there's no reason we all have to wait for someone else to build what we have in mind.

There is, however, one thing I want to avoid: *feature creep*.

Not every special requirement needs another checkbox, settings page, or submenu. Whenever possible, I'd rather keep the core simple and offer **hooks and extension points** for project-specific behavior.

In short: flexible enough for developers, understandable enough for everyone else. Ideally without needing a user manual the size of a phone book.

## Open source means open for business, too

The plugins in this organization are intended to be released under the **GPLv2 License**, because of Wordpress license. That means you're welcome to use, modify, redistribute, and incorporate them into commercial projects, subject to the license terms.

I also believe AI-assisted code should be shared openly wherever practical. AI systems have benefited from an enormous amount of human-created knowledge and content. Making useful results available to others feels like a reasonable way to give something back.

That's a personal principle, not a claim that AI-generated code is automatically free of copyright or licensing obligations. Dependencies and third-party code still need to be handled properly.

## Want to help?

Try a plugin. Report a bug. Share an idea — or use AI to build it the way you envision it and open a pull request. The important part isn't who typed the code, but whether the result is useful, understandable, and reliable.

Constructive feedback and contributions are always welcome.

**Donations? No thanks.** 🐾

I'm not accepting donations for these projects. If you feel like giving something back, your local animal shelter would probably appreciate the support far more than my GitHub account would.

Thanks for stopping by — and happy building!
